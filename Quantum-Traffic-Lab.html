04   Inside the model & circuit

The same problem, two methods

Bit order is |q₀q₁⟩ = 00, 01, 10, 11. Each string selects one feasible timing plan. The cycle is always 68 s: NS green + 4 s clearance + EW green + 4 s clearance. Greens sum to 60 s, with a 10 s minimum. Conflicting greens never overlap.

Every run begins empty at NS green and lasts 600 s. Uniform deterministic arrivals use an accumulator. Arrivals join before service each second; green service uses a fractional credit at 0.5 vehicles/s. Unused credit is cleared when empty or red. Delay accumulates the queue remaining after service each second, including vehicles still waiting at the horizon. No turning, pedestrians, spillback, yellow-phase behavior, or car-following physics are modeled.

Cost Hamiltonian

C = wᴅ D/60 + wᴘ Qmax/30 + wʀ R/30

D: total queued vehicle-seconds / total arrivals (0 when empty); Qmax: largest single-axis queue before service; R: vehicles still queued at 600 s. These are fixed scales, not data-dependent normalization. Weights need not sum to one.

H = diag(C₀₀, C₀₁, C₁₀, C₁₁). For stable angle scales, phase evolution uses H̃ = (H − Cmin I)/(Cmax − Cmin); if tied, H̃ = 0. This preserves all minimizers. Reported expectations always use original C.

|00⟩ → H⊗H → [ exp(−iγₗH̃) → Rx(2βₗ)⊗Rx(2βₗ) ]ᵖ → measure

Angles appear after training.

Four complex amplitudes are evolved exactly. Classical Nelder–Mead minimizes ⟨H⟩ without shot noise. Finite multistart training is heuristic and may miss the best parameters. Probabilities are exact, not sampled counts.

A learning experiment, not a speedup claim. For four plans, exhaustive classical search is simpler and exact. This simulator demonstrates QAOA on a precomputed objective; it does not accelerate the traffic simulation or run on quantum hardware.
